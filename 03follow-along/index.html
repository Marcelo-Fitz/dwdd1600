Declaration of Indapendance

What it Says

The Declaration of Independence, written in 1776, explains why the American colonies decided to separate from Great Britain and become an independent nation. It states that all people are created equal and have natural rights, including life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness. The document argues that governments exist to protect these rights and that people have the right to change or remove a government that does not do so. It also lists many complaints against King George III to justify the colonies’ decision to declare independence.

Why we are Upset

  1. King George III imposed taxes on the colonists without giving them representation in the British government.
  2. The British government passed unfair laws that limited the colonists’ rights and self-government.
  3. British soldiers were stationed in the colonies, and colonists were often forced to provide them with housing and supplies.
